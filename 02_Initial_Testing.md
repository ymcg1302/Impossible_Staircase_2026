## Process and Takeaways

In the previous stage, I took note of 3 prevailing structures that I could base my Chessboard Staircase on:

1. The Literal Staircase Chessboard approach = using references found online, I saw that although the original artist is unknown, an original copy of it can be found in the [Gökyay Chess Museum](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g298656-d8735226-Reviews-Gokyay_Chess_Museum-Ankara.html#/media/8735226/805653547:p/?type=ALL_INCLUDING_RESTRICTED&albumid=-160&category=-160) in Ankara, Turkey where the Gökyay Foundation was established to encourage the love of chess among youth. The design was popularized online and has been remade by many people online. 

2. The Layered Platform Chessboard approach = here the reference integrated a multi-tiered approach with less of a focus on the staircase and more the rhythm created between the different steps more similar to the [Ascending and Descending](https://www.nancyshahani.com/professor/student_work/student_sites/suny/one/patt/index.html?utm_source=Pinterest&utm_medium=organic) lithograph done by Escher. 

3. The last approach would be more similar del Prete's approach to illusion, alongside another reference found online by an Optical Illusion Fan, Artist, and Crop Scientist, [Ben van der Zee](https://www.facebook.com/groups/mcescher/posts/10158969820562197/?ref=fb_answers).

### Test 1: Staircases Only → Too Much, Too Impossible?

The first pass actually combined structures 1 and 2 as I thought that their construction would be similar. To make this simpler, I found out about the Duplicate Special tool as a suggestion both from AI and external sources [here](https://help.autodesk.com/view/MAYAUL/2022/ENU/?guid=GUID-961B262B-F882-452C-B6A9-1B7F3AD8E0FE) and [here](https://youtu.be/u7mS8LrshWk?si=PmhP_0prftvDnBD-). The implementation was very straightforward as I started with a cube in the center of the world, set the pivot to the bottom of the square using Snap to Points which I then scaled up. 

From there, I used the Duplicate Special tool to create copies in the desired direction and reduced their scale each time. The original settings were: 
* Translate in X, Y, or Z by 1 depending on the direction of the output
* Scale the Y axis by 0.9 - 0.95 each time (as a percentage) depending on the desired distance so it would decrease each time
* Lastly, make the number of copies of 7 to emulate the chessboard dimensions. 

There were quite a few overlapping cubes as I created them so I had to delete them by hand. 

After this, to attempt the second structure, it was much simpler as I just used the Snap to Points again to align the top faces with its neighbors on each row. However, in comparing the descended corners, there were irregularities with the structure that I felt would break the illusion causing me to take a step back and try a different approach.

### Test 2: Illusion Only → Staircase Present, but where's the structure? 

Trying to recreate the work from a different lens, I decided to focus solely on creating the outer rim of the staircase to resemble Penrose' with one step eliminated near the end to preserve the illusion. I also maintained the 8-step structure for a chessboard but had to compromise this as the illusion approached the loop's end. I also had to ask, where would the chessboard be placed? While most of the examples we were shown did have an empty middle to keep the staircase as the main focus, I thought that my idea could be implemented more explicitly, which is what I attempted in my third and final structure test.

### Test 3: Combining Both → Procedural Algorithm Found?

For my last structure test, I did 2 things: (1) I double checked the brief to understand what aspects of proceduralism were considered and whether or not I needed to implement the illusion itself or simply the staircase. In this case, I was able to do so with the guidance of another artist mentioned above. 

I also approached the construction differently. I started with the cube in the center of the world and rotated it 45 degrees to see if I can start with the same structure as the reference and to see if I could eliminate the need to delete the inner cubes by hand. In doing so, I made something similar to a procedural or process based approaching to placing and creating the cubes/steps.
